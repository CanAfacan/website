Standard-cell characterization at Arm

On Arm’s Source Team, I evaluated new standard-cell topologies for low-power computing. I compared them with existing library cells and built transistor-level simulation setups for 41 new circuits and macros.

Role
Source Team intern

Electrical characterization

I used Cadence Virtuoso to prepare the source collateral for three types of analysis.

  • Sequential-cell margins. I developed high-sigma Monte Carlo simulations for flip-flop margining and prepared separate tests for latch-node stability.
  • Power integrity. I built setups for electromigration and IR-drop (EMIR) analysis.
  • Transistor networks. I prepared balanced beta-ratio and channel-connected block analyses for the new topologies.

Simulation automation

One test setup for three simulators

I wrote a converter that takes a Spectre simulation setup and generates its HSPICE and Analog FastSPICE (AFS) equivalents. That let me update a test once in Spectre and carry the change into all three simulators.

Checking a cell against its intended behavior

I built a graphical tool that generates HSPICE input-vector files, runs the simulation, and reports how far the outputs deviate from the expected responses. This gave the team a way to test whether a new transistor topology implemented the intended logic.

Verilog modeling

I developed 13 structural and behavioral Verilog models to represent cell behavior in logic-level simulation.
